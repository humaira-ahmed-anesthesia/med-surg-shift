# Float Pool

*Formerly Med-Surg Shift.* You're a float pool nurse sent wherever the hospital needs you: Med-Surg, Rapid Response, the ER, the ICU, Labor & Delivery, Postpartum, Pediatrics, the PICU and the NICU.

A cozy nursing time-management game, in the spirit of cooking games like Cooking Fever, but the "recipes" are real nursing interventions.

**Play:** open `index.html` in a browser, or play it on GitHub Pages once that's turned on.

## How it plays
- You're the nurse on 4 West with 3 patients. A 12-hour shift (07:00–19:00) lasts about 3 minutes.
- A guided tutorial, then three floor levels (Day shift on 4 West, Busy Monday, Short-staffed Saturday), each with its own patients and emergencies.
- **ER (10 levels):** 3 bays growing to 6, patients arriving all shift from a pool of about 30 real ER presentations (abdominal pain that turns out to be appendicitis or an ectopic, sepsis, DKA, overdoses with a call to poison control, a precipitous delivery, a farm injury needing a tourniquet, STEMI, stroke, intubations). You do the nursing actions; the MD's steps (ultrasound, intubation, stitches, delivery) play out in the feed. Call for help brings the whole team. Three hearts: lose one when a patient dies, and they come back one every 30 minutes.
- **ICU (short tutorial + 14 levels):** one patient, all shift. Titrate drips with − / + to keep a number in its goal range (norepinephrine for MAP, insulin for glucose, propofol for sedation, nicardipine, 3% saline, nitroglycerin, epinephrine, and hourly Parkland-style fluids for burns). Hourly cares come due on a clock (EVD level & zero, chest tube checks, oral care, turns, burn dressings with premedication), and alarms interrupt (vent alarms → call RT, ICP spikes → call neurosurgery, tamponade, escharotomy, carbon monoxide and cyanide). Levels cover medical-surgical, trauma, neuro, cardiac (including an Impella and a balloon pump) and burn ICUs, plus ECMO and a vascular ICU with a spinal drain. The patient's main device (ECMO, Impella, balloon pump, CRRT, EVD, vent and so on) shows as a little drawing beside their face. The H&P has a Lines & drains tab explaining every device. Three patients die despite everything; that never costs a heart or points, and in two of those levels a new admission arrives afterward.
- **Labor & Delivery (5 levels):** 2 labor rooms, and only 1 patient when someone is critical. A scrolling fetal monitor shows the heart rate and category. Epidurals and the drop in BP after them, oxytocin tachysystole and terbutaline, fever in labor, preeclampsia and magnesium (with magnesium toxicity and calcium gluconate), eclampsia, shoulder dystocia with McRoberts and suprapubic pressure, newborn bag-mask breaths, cord prolapse, abruption, uterine rupture, a maternal code with left uterine displacement, and postpartum hemorrhage where you choose a uterotonic that's safe for her history.
- **Postpartum (5 levels):** 3 mother-baby rooms. Fundus checks, latching, newborn meds, RhoGAM, baby glucose checks, urinary retention, spinal headaches, jaundice, depression screening, postpartum hemorrhage, postpartum preeclampsia, endometritis and a pulmonary embolism.
- **Pediatrics (5 levels):** infants to teens with weight-based doses worked out from each child's weight, and a "Which dose?" check that catches 10× errors. Asthma, croup, RSV, new and teen type 1 diabetes with lows, sickle cell crisis and acute chest, Kawasaki disease, fever in a newborn, anaphylaxis, seizures.
- **PICU (5 levels):** plays like the ICU with kid-sized drips (mcg/kg/min): septic shock, DKA with the two-bag dextrose system and brain swelling, a head injury with an EVD, status asthmaticus on terbutaline, and a 5-month-old after heart surgery.
- **NICU (5 levels):** 2 babies. Adjust each isolette to keep the baby's temperature 36.5–37.5 °C. Feeding, CPAP, caffeine, brady spells, jaundice, low sugars, a sepsis rule-out, NEC, a pneumothorax and a resuscitation at delivery.

**All hands:** during a code, a massive transfusion or another big emergency, you stay in that room. Coworkers cover your other patients (their care pauses and picks back up afterward).

**Unlocking:** each unit's first level is open; the next level needs at least 1 star on the one before it. Rapid Response opens once every ER and ICU level has a star. Med-Surg has 10 shifts, and the ER has its own orientation.

**Float shift and Overtime:** Float sends you to a random unit with a random mix of its patients. Overtime is an endless ER shift that keeps getting busier until 3 patients are lost (died or left without being seen).

**Your nurse and Poppy pins:** name your nurse and pick skin, hair and scrubs. Every 10 exceptional shifts (3 stars, nobody lost) earns a Poppy nomination with a thank-you letter and a pin for that unit. Sometimes a patient's family drops off food for the unit.

**Orientation and rank:** you start as a new grad on orientation. Every point from every shift counts toward your rank: New Grad, Novice Nurse, Advanced Beginner, Competent Nurse, Proficient Nurse, Preceptor, Charge Nurse, Expert Nurse and Float Pool Legend (inspired by Benner's novice-to-expert model).
- **Rapid Response Nurse:** one patient at a time across the hospital. Run a pulseless VT code and an asystole/PEA code from hyperkalemia (CPR cycles you can fast-forward, shocks, epinephrine, amiodarone, H's and T's), a stroke to CT and TNK, and a liver transplant hemorrhage with massive transfusion (whole blood, PRBCs, plasma, platelets, cryo) before rushing to surgery. Call for more help brings the code team, stroke team or blood bank. If you're too slow, the patient expires.
- A vitals monitor refreshes on its own. Abnormal numbers turn red, and some emergencies start with a vitals check.
- Assess every patient once a shift (an orange ring means not yet). Some problems start with an assessment, like a respiratory exam before a neb.
- Each patient has a problem and a short chain of care (for example, low hemoglobin: type & screen → large-bore IV → hang PRBCs). The next step glows; tap the matching tool at the bottom. The feed lists what everyone needs, but you have to go to each room yourself.
- Health bars drain while patients wait. Tap a patient on the right to go to their room.
- Emergencies happen: desats, bleeding, sepsis, opioid oversedation, A-fib with RVR, allergic and transfusion reactions, hypotension, wound dehiscence, strokes, STEMIs and severe hypoglycemia.
- Some patients need the ICU no matter what. Call the rapid response team or charge to escalate in time.
- Some emergencies need more than the floor: rush them to the OR, CT or the cath lab.
- Each patient has an H&P folder (history of present illness, home meds, allergies, social history, exam, plan) and a log of the shift. The game pauses while it's open.
- Earn slow moments by sending patients home or getting them where they need to go. Tap the snail to slow time for 15 seconds.
